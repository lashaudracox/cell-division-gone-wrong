Analyze real survival data and a real CAR-T clinical trial, then think like a trial designer

Level 1 · Read the Data~8 min
1. By how many percentage points did 5-year survival rise from 1990–1994 to 2000–2005? About how many more children out of every 100 survived?ANALYZE
2. The study's lead author said most of this gain came from "using existing drugs better," not from new drugs. Using what you learned in Section 5, how can clinical trials improve survival without inventing a new drug?ANALYZE
🧪 Part 2 · Evaluating a CAR-T Trial
In a Phase 2 trial at 25 hospitals in 11 countries, 75 children and young adults whose B-cell ALL had relapsed or resisted treatment received one CAR-T infusion. Every patient in the trial got CAR-T; there was no comparison group.
ResultNumber
Went into remission within 3 months61 of 75
Alive 12 months after infusion76%
Had cytokine release syndrome (strong immune reaction)77%

Source: Maude SL, Laetsch TW, Buechner J, et al. Tisagenlecleucel in children and young adults with B-cell lymphoblastic leukemia. New England Journal of Medicine. 2018;378(5):439–448.

Level 2 · Evaluate~10 min
3. Calculate the remission rate (61 ÷ 75). These patients had already failed other treatments. Why does that make this result especially impressive?EVALUATE
4. A news site concludes: "CAR-T is better than chemotherapy for all children with leukemia." Name two reasons this trial cannot support that claim.EVALUATE

5. Design a Phase 3 trial that could test whether CAR-T should be used earlier, for children with high-risk ALL at diagnosis. Describe who you would enroll, what the two groups would receive, and what you would measure. Name one safety concern your trial would need to watch.CREATE
