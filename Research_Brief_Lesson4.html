Research Brief: A Treatment Strategy for Leo

Use everything from all four lessons to recommend a plan for a new patient you haven't seen before

Your role: You are a junior researcher presenting to Leo's tumor board. Your brief must explain what's broken in Leo's cells, why it causes cancer, and what treatment strategy the evidence supports. You may use your notes, the Scaffolded Diagrams, the Designer Reference Guide, and the portal. Write in your own words.

Leo M., age 14

T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) · Newly diagnosed · Slow early response

Clinical history

Diagnosed after a cough and trouble breathing, caused by a mass in his chest. Started combination chemotherapy. After the first month, leukemia cells were still detectable in his bone marrow, a sign of higher risk.

Leukemia cell surface

CD19: negative. T-cell markers present.

Gene notes & family

NOTCH1 sends a growth signal that tells young T cells to keep dividing. CDKN2A makes p16, a brake that holds the G1/S checkpoint closed. No cancers in the family. No germline variants found.
